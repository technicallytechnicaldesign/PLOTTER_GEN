computing

How it goes together. Six parts: the body (with the head), two bent arms, two legs and the shout bubble. Stack them from the front: the body, then the legs and arms behind it on split pins A to D, and last of all the bubble, right at the back, on pin E through the nose (or the brow). The bubble has to be the back layer, because on its swing it passes the shoulder pins and would catch on their shanks. Tie one thread across the two arm tabs and one across the two leg tabs; tie a pull string to the middle of the arm thread, run it down past the feet and knot the middle of the leg thread onto it on the way. Then tie a short thread from the bubble's tab to the arm knot, just taut with the ogre at rest. Hang it from the hole in the head. Pull, and the arms swing up with their bend turned into a claw over the head while the bubble comes out from behind. The rest pose in the preview is worked out from each part's weight (the centre of its area hangs under its pin) and the pulled pose from the tab geometry: an arm turns until its tab points straight down, the bubble's tab is cut to the length that makes it finish on the same pull, and the legs' longer tabs turn them only by the kick you set. All of that is geometry, not yet a test: card stiffness, pin friction and thread stretch are ASSUMED negligible until the first one is built. Cut sizes are ASSUMED until the cut calibration sheet is read: pin hole 2.4 mm for a standard brass paper fastener, at least 3 mm of card round every hole, no part narrower than 5 mm, 3 mm between parts, ink gap 0.4 mm. The pen draws first and stops a small gap short of every cut line, then the blade cuts on the same mat load.
