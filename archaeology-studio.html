Archaeology studio

Four kinds of archaeological drawing, laid out the way a site report plate is laid out: a trench section with numbered contexts and the cuts that truncate them, a site plan with contours, walls, postholes and findspots, a sieve tray of finds, and a pot refitted from its sherds. Export a millimetre SVG with up to three pen layers for Design Space.

Split export saves one SVG per pen (key line, shade, colour), numbered in plotting order. Every file carries the same four corner ticks so Design Space sizes them identically: place each file at the same spot on the mat, swap pens between runs, and leave the paper on the mat.

computing

Trench section: deposits are numbered contexts, each hatched with its own convention (dense stipple for topsoil, fine stipple for sand, short dashes for clay, pebble outlines for gravel, cross-hatch for burnt layers, angular outlines for rubble). Cuts are drawn in the colour pen and truncate only the deposits older than them, while younger deposits run unbroken across the top; where a younger cut is dug through an older fill, the older lines stop at its edge. Tip-lines inside fills taper to the cut edge, and the artefacts are drawn a little larger than true scale so they read at 1:20. Site plan: 1 mm is 250 mm on the ground (1:250). Walls are double lines with jointed masonry, dashed where robbed out and open at doors; postholes are circles, hatched when excavated; the ditch is a pair of dashed lines; findspots are triangles (flint), circles (pottery), squares (metal) and stars (coin), each kept clear of walls and of every other symbol and number. Sieve tray: the mesh is drawn first and every find hides the wire beneath it; nothing overlaps. Pottery refit: the left half is the outside of the vessel with its decoration and the fracture lines between sherds, the right half is the wall in section, and dashed outlines in the colour pen mark sherds that have not been found. In Design Space: send all layers to Pen, black 0.4 for the key line, black 0.3 for shade and texture, and a coloured pen for the colour layer, then Attach and Make.
