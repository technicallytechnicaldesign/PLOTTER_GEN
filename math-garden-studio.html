Math garden studio

Three ways to grow a drawing out of a rule. A harmonograph swings two damped pendulums against each other until the pen spirals to a stop. An L-system rewrites a word over and over and lets a turtle walk it as plants, snowflakes and space-filling curves. A Mandelbrot or Julia set is drawn as contour lines, the way a map draws hills.

Pendulums

A ratio of small whole numbers closes into a Lissajous figure (some phases fold it back on itself into a single arc); detune makes it walk round, and the fade pulls it in towards the middle the way a real pendulum loses swing.

L-system

Each generation rewrites every letter by its rule. F draws a step, + and - turn, [ and ] start and end a branch. Generations past the symbol cap stop early and say so.

Contours

Each point of the page is a number c; the page counts how fast z becomes z squared plus c runs away. The lines join points that run away equally fast, so they crowd in where the edge of the set is busiest.

computing

How it works: the harmonograph is a sum of decaying sines, sampled finely enough that no step is longer than half a millimetre before the line is simplified. The L-system expands its word up to 600,000 symbols and walks it with a turtle. The contours come from a smooth escape-time count on a grid across the page, traced with marching squares, with the levels set either by area or evenly in escape time. Pen widths and plotting time are ASSUMED, the same as the other studios.
