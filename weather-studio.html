Weather drawn two ways. The sky view is a landscape of hill ridges under clouds of five real kinds, with rain, snow, hail, lightning, wind, fog, sun and rainbow. The chart view is a 1970s forecast map with isobars, fronts, wind barbs and a coastline. Export a millimetre SVG with up to three pen layers for Design Space.

computing

The sky view sorts everything by distance. Ridges are noise curves, and each nearer ridge hides the ones behind it. Cumulus and cumulonimbus are unions of ellipses outlined as one silhouette with a flat base, and the underside is hatched more densely toward the base. Stratus is a stack of wavy lines that shorten toward the ends, cirrus follows a noise flow field and curls at the tips, and altocumulus is rows of small puffs that shrink toward the horizon. Every line is tested against the clouds and ridges in front of it, so a sun, a rainbow, a shower or a bolt is cut away where something nearer covers it. Rain and snow fall from under a cloud base and stop at the ridge they land on. The chart view builds a pressure field from seeded Gaussian highs and lows, traces isobars with marching squares, cuts a gap in each for its number, and draws the fronts along the lowest-pressure route out of each low. Wind barbs lie along the isobars with low pressure on the left, and speed comes from the pressure gradient (an artistic scale, not calibrated).
