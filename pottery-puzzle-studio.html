Pottery puzzle studio

A decorated pot is drawn by the pen and then cut by the blade into sherds on the same mat, so the card becomes a refit puzzle that carries its own drawing. The decoration follows traditional motifs and marks (comb-stamped bands, cord impressions, meanders, ovolo, fingertip cordons), drawn flat and wrapped round the elevation of the pot. Export one SVG with pen layers and a red cut layer for Design Space, or the reassembly board that goes with it.

Paper and pens

Split export saves one SVG per layer in job order: the pen layers, then the cut. The pen files carry four corner ticks at 1 mm and the cut file carries a trim rectangle at 1 mm, so every file has the same bounding box and lands in the same place in Design Space.

computing

How it goes together: the pot is a surface of revolution drawn in orthographic elevation, split into rim, neck, shoulder, belly and lower body at landmarks of its profile. Each zone holds one or more bands of motifs, laid out flat in angle and height and wrapped on with x = r(y) sin(theta), so marks narrow towards the sides the way they do on a real pot. The card is then broken into sherds by sequential fractures: each break is a wavy line across one piece, chosen so both halves stay big enough to cut, none has a neck thinner than the limit, and no corner is sharper than the limit; the breaks lean towards the zone boundaries and round the circumference when the bias slider is up. Decoration stops a small gap short of every cut line. In Design Space: upload the one SVG, set each pen layer to Draw with the pen you will load (black 0.4, black 0.3, and the colour pen), set the red layer to Cut with the card setting, select everything and Attach so the layers keep their places, then Make. The Explore 5 draws every pen layer first, then you pull the pen and it cuts, all on one mat load, so ink and blade stay in register; do not move the mat between the two. Every cut size here is an assumption until the cut calibration sheet has been run and measured: smallest piece 5 mm inscribed radius, no neck under 6 mm, no corner under 40 degrees, no piece longer than 2.6 times its width, edge wiggle up to 2 mm, ink gap 0.4 mm. Shared cracks are cut once from each side, which is assumed to be acceptable on card. Each tradition is drawn in the manner of, from the kinds of mark that are known on that pottery, and no piece is a copy of a real pot.
