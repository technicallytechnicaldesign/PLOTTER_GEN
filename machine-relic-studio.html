Machine relic

A relic for Inktober day 2. Old hardware kept like a saint's bone: a chip, a memory stick, a broken board or a valve hangs in preserving fluid inside a caged jar. Hoses and cables run out of it, wax seals hold strips of litany to the glass, and a cog, a halo or sacred geometry stands behind it, ringed with binary scripture.

Ink

Outline passes after the second only go on the shadow side, lower right. Dark areas are hatched in the line pen, never filled solid: 0.75 mm crosshatch read as an even dark tone on paper, and under 0.5 mm the lines flood and soak the paper. Whip shading and fluid tone are stippled dots in the thin pen.

Split export saves one SVG per pen in plotting order: colours first, then the thin pen, then the black line last so it sits on top. Each file carries four corner ticks at 1 mm, so they all land in the same place.

computing

How it is made: every part is a distance field in millimetres, drawn front to back so each outline stops where something stands in front of it. The jar is a glass box with a cap stack (lip, ribbed collar, bolt band, dome, fitting and hanging ring) and a base stack (lip, bolt band, taper and a finial or stand). The relic hangs in the glass on its own rotated frame and sits behind the cage bars and the bubbles but in front of the back of the fluid. Cables are tubes along cubic curves that start behind the jar. Pens are 0.4 mm for the line and colours and 0.3 mm for the thin pen (ASSUMED, as on the apple), none plotted yet.
