Underground studio

A cut-away slice of the earth, like a plate from an old natural history book: grass, flowers, mushrooms and a tree above the ground line, and below it soil layers, stones, roots that bend around them, a web of mycelium, burrows, worms, a fossil and a lost key. Export a millimetre SVG with two or three pen layers for Design Space.

Split export saves one SVG per pen (key line, shade, colour), numbered in plotting order. Every file carries the same four corner ticks so Design Space sizes them identically: place each file at the same spot on the mat, swap pens between runs, and leave the paper on the mat.

The ground line is a fractal noise curve, and the soil below it is cut into three to six layers whose boundaries wander and sometimes pinch out to nothing. Each layer has its own texture on the shade pen: dots, short dashes, wavy laminations, crosshatched clay, packed pebbles, and blocky bedrock. Stones are drawn on top of everything: nothing is drawn inside a stone or a burrow. Roots are grown by space colonisation, a set of food points scattered in the soil that pull the nearest root tip toward them, denser near the surface and much wider under a tree. Growth steps that would enter a stone are turned aside, so roots wrap around rocks. Thick roots are two tapering outlines on the key pen with fine hairs at the tips. The mycelium is a random walk with persistence and branching that starts at the mushrooms and at the roots and ends on a root tip when it finds one. In monolith mode the slice becomes a block seen from above and to the right, with a hatched side face. In core mode several cylinders are drawn at their true depths, each showing the strata wrapped round it. In Design Space: every layer to Pen, black 0.4 for the key line, black or grey 0.3 for the shade, any colour 0.3 for the colour layer, Attach, Make.
