Spooky garland

Ghosts, pumpkins and bats to cut out and string across a window. Every charm has a small hanging hole at the top (or two, so the string weaves through and they all face the room), and the faces are jack-o-lantern faces: drawn by the pen and filled in dark, or cut right through so the light shines out of them. Every seed is a new set of faces.

The charms
Faces

Cut-through faces glow best with yellow tissue or baking paper glued behind them. A cut face that would leave a strip of card thinner than 2 mm fails the gates below; make the charms bigger if it does.

Hanging

One hole: tie each charm on with a short loop of thread. Two holes: thread the string in at the back, out through one hole and back in through the other, so the charms sit flat and face the room.

Paper and pens

Split export saves one SVG per layer in job order: the pen layers, then the cut. The pen files carry four corner ticks at 1 mm and the cut file carries a trim rectangle at 1 mm, so every file lands in the same place in Design Space.

computing

How it is made: each charm is a set of rounded shapes (pumpkin lobes, a ghost's dome and wavy hem, a bat's scalloped wings) merged into one outline by a distance field, and the cut line is where that field crosses zero. A cut-through face is subtracted from the same field, so the eyes and mouth come out as clean holes, and the gates check that every strip of card left between them and the edge is at least 2 mm wide (assumed, not yet cut-tested). The hanging hole always keeps 2.2 mm of card round it (assumed). Pumpkins hang from their stems.
