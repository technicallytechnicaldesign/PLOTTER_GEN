Cactus

Cacti grown by their own rule for Inktober day 4. Every areole turns the golden angle from the last, so the ribs and spirals come out in Fibonacci numbers by themselves. Five scenes share one plant: a specimen plate with its herbarium label, a saguaro that ages from seedling to standing bones, a windowsill of potted cacti, a desert night with an owl in a nest hole, and a prickly pear with its cochineal and the carmine they make.

Years grows the plant: a barrel fattens, a saguaro puts out arms after 30, blooms in its prime, then past 70 the skin tears and the woody ribs show, and by 90 only the ribs stand, some fallen.

Growth

Ribs of 5, 8, 13, 21 or 34 are the ones real cacti settle on. Neighbouring ribs step their areoles by the golden ratio, which lines them up into the spirals you see.

Flowers and fruit

Cochineal are scale insects that live on the pads under tufts of white wax. Crushed, they give carmine: the red pen draws their colonies, and the swatch and the bug on the plate say where the colour comes from.

Ink

Rib shading runs along the ribs on the shadow side, never across them, so a narrow rib never fills with X marks. Dark areas (holes, shadows, the night) are hatched in the line pen, never filled solid: under 0.5 mm the lines flood.

computing

How it is made: a cactus is a few limbs, each a centreline with a radius along it (a barrel is one fat limb with a flat dome, a saguaro a trunk with arms that leave sideways and turn up). Ribs are lines at fixed angles round each limb, seen from the front, so they crowd toward the edges. Areoles sit on the ribs and step by the golden ratio from rib to rib; spines leave each areole in 3D and are projected, so the ones at the edge point out sideways. Prickly pear pads carry their areoles on a sunflower spiral (Vogel's model), and an echeveria grows its leaves the same way. A dying saguaro loses its skin in patches to a noise field and shows the woody rods inside; past 85 years the rods splay, break and fall. Pens are 0.4 mm for the line and colours and 0.3 mm for the thin pen (ASSUMED), none plotted yet.
