Paper doll wardrobe

Clothes, hair and hats for the plain doll from the jointed figure studio. Each piece of clothing sits on one part of the doll and moves with it: where it covers a joint it has a pin hole at exactly that joint and goes on the same split pin, and where it does not, it has fold tabs that wrap behind the doll. Set the doll's size and joints to match the doll you cut, or load that doll's SVG.

Paper and pens

Split export saves one SVG per layer in job order: the pen layers, then the cut. The pen files carry four corner ticks at 1 mm and the cut file carries a trim rectangle at 1 mm.

computing

How it goes together: every piece is the doll part underneath grown by 1.2 mm and trimmed to the garment's shape, so it covers the doll with a small margin. A piece that covers a joint gets a disc of card round a pin hole at exactly that joint: take the split pin out, stack the doll part and its clothes, and push the pin back through all of them, doll at the back. The assembly letters say which hole goes where (A and B shoulders, C and D elbows, E and F hips, G and H knees, N the neck). A piece that covers fewer than two joints, like a short sleeve, a shoe or the hair, has fold tabs marked with a dotted fold line: fold them back round the doll part. With a skirt, a dress or a long coat, pin the legs behind the body so the skirt hangs over them. Every cut size here is an assumption until the cut calibration sheet is read, as in the jointed figure studio, and how many layers one brass fastener can hold is untested: the stats show the thickest joint.
