Battering ram studio

Inktober day 9, ram. A siege ram to fold up: a shed with a gable roof on painted wheels, and the beam that hangs through it with a ram's head on the end. Plain wood, or the ram all over it (a skull over the door, a fleece of curls on the roof, horns down the beam), or RAM: circuit-board walls and a beam that is a memory stick.

Job order: colour pens, shade, line, score, then the cut. Pen files carry corner ticks; the cut file a trim rectangle at 1 mm.

computing

How it goes together. The shed: fold the walls up off the floor, the roof slopes over off the walls and glue the ridge, then fold each gable up and glue its flaps inside the walls and roof. Slide the beam through the square holes in both gables, head out of the front: the holes are a millimetre bigger than the beam so it slides. The wheels and horns are cut separately: push a split pin through each, then through its hole in the wall or the beam, and open the legs inside before closing up, so the wheels roll and the horns can be posed. Built on the Smack studio's net engine. Cut and fold sizes untested.
