computing

How it works: the letters come from a stroke font built into this page, and each stroke is thickened by the letter weight. The page measures the distance from every point of a fine grid to that shape. The wheel's centre runs along the line where that distance equals the wheel radius plus the gap, which is exactly the path a real wheel's centre follows when it rolls against the outline, and it is why a big wheel cannot get into the narrow gaps a small one can. A rolling wheel turns once for every circumference of travel of its centre, so the pen sits at the centre plus the hole distance, turned by the distance travelled over the radius. The radius is nudged by a fraction of a percent so the pen comes back to its exact start after the chosen number of laps and every lap falls in a different place, which is what builds the woven band. Inside the outline the wheel turns the other way. Breathe slowly swells and shrinks the hole over the whole drawing. Lots of laps put a lot of ink in one place: the pen length is shown above, and very dense settings can soak thin paper.
