computing

How it works: the letters come from a stroke font built into this page, thickened by the letter weight; shapes are outlines. The page measures the distance from every point of a fine grid to the shape, and a wheel's centre runs along the line where that distance equals the wheel radius plus the gap, which is the path a real wheel's centre follows against the outline, so a big wheel skips the narrow gaps a small one gets into. Wheel size is a share of the shape: half the letter height for letters, the shape's radius for shapes. A round wheel turns once for every circumference its centre travels; the pen sits at the centre plus the hole distance, turned by that angle. A lobed roller (oval, triangle, square, five lobes, wavy) has a radius that changes as it turns, so its centre bobs towards and away from the track and it turns faster on its flat sides and slower on its long ones; the turn is solved exactly so the pattern still closes. Each radius is nudged by a fraction of a percent so the pen comes back to its exact start after the chosen laps, with every lap in a new place. Inside the outline the wheel turns the other way. Scatter packs a seeded garden of shapes onto the page, and orbit rings small ones round a big one; variety lets each graph wander from the layer settings in wheel, hole, laps, roller and pen. Lots of laps put a lot of ink in one place: the pen length is shown above, and very dense settings can soak thin paper.
