Poison apple

A poison apple for Inktober. The poured coat is the skull: eye sockets and a nose open up as holes in it, the dark apple shows through them, and the coat runs off the jaw in drips like teeth. One slider takes it from a plain candy apple to a rotten, melting skull. Black ink like a sticker, plain lines like a colouring page, or a colour tattoo with a poison-green coat over a red apple.

Ink

Outline passes after the second only go on the shadow side, lower right. Dark areas are hatched in the line pen, never filled solid: 0.75 mm crosshatch read as an even dark tone on paper, and under 0.5 mm the lines flood and soak the paper. Whip shading is stippled dots in the thin pen on the coat's shadow side.

How it is made: two layers. The apple underneath is a polar outline with two shoulders, a stem dimple and a base that narrows with Heart shape. The coat on top is one distance field: everything above a jaw edge that climbs toward the cheeks, smoothly merged with a row of drips that hang off that edge, lumped out over the sides of the apple, minus two tilted sockets and a nose that points up. Wherever the coat is not, the apple shows. Bone ridges are offset contours of the sockets, kept to short arcs. Pens are 0.4 mm for the line and colours (the maker's) and 0.3 mm for the thin pen (ASSUMED), none plotted yet.
