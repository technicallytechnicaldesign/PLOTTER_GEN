Smack

Nets that fold up into something, for Inktober day 5. A cube that took a hit: the burst, the speed lines and the cracks run over its edges, so they only join up once it is folded, and the other five faces carry their own sound effects so it rolls as a die. Or a lantern wrapped in a smack of jellyfish, with the bells cut out to let the light through. Both run on one net engine. It unfolds the solid, adds the glue tabs and draws the art on the folded surface, so every seam lines up.

The box

Size over 100 lets the burst spill over the edges of the front face. The drop shadow and the letter blocks are shaded with lines along their length, so a 2 mm band never fills with X marks.

Rays and cracks

Rays and cracks are drawn on the folded cube, out from the front face over the four edges round it. Where one crosses the glued seam it is split between the two ends of the net and joins again when you fold it.

The other faces

Five words, in order: right, top, left, bottom, back. Capital letters, numbers and ! ? - . ' are drawn.

The lantern

A bell only becomes a window if it sits wholly inside one face with 3 mm of card round it; the rest are drawn. Light it with an LED tea light, never a flame.

The jellyfish
Ink

Dark areas (a jelly's shadow side, a hatched backdrop, hatched water if chosen) are hatched in the line pen, never filled solid: under 0.5 mm the lines flood. Run-off thins the hatch out toward the light.

Pens
Paper

Split export saves one SVG per layer in running order: colours, the thin pen, the line pen, then the score, then the cut. Pen and score files carry corner ticks at 1 mm; the cut file carries a trim rectangle at 1 mm instead, so every file has the same box.

computing

How it is made: the solid is a prism (a cube is a four-sided prism as tall as it is wide), with each face carrying its own flat frame and its place in 3D. The net is a tree of faces: each child turns about the edge it shares with its parent until it lies flat, and every edge the tree leaves open gets a glue tab, a tuck flap or a cut. Art is drawn in a development, a flat map of several faces at once: the cube's front face with its four neighbours folded down round it, or the lantern's walls rolled out into one strip that wraps round on itself. Every line is cut where it crosses from one face to the next, so a ray or a hatch line that leaves one end of the net comes back in at the other and meets itself when folded; the seam gate measures that in 3D. Ink stops 0.4 mm short of every cut (ASSUMED, as in the jointed figure studio), glue tabs are 7 mm, and the fold and perforation sizes are ASSUMED until the cut calibration sheet is read. Pens are 0.4 mm for the line and colours and 0.3 mm for the thin pen (ASSUMED), none plotted yet.
