Spider web studio

Webs grown in the order a spider builds them: a bridge thread, a frame hung from twigs and grass, radii from a hub that sits above the middle, then the sticky spiral laid from the outside in with turn-backs in the roomy lower half. Every seed comes out a little lopsided. Export a millimetre SVG with two or three pen layers for Design Space.

Each seed builds a web the way a spider would. First the twigs or grass stems come in from the page edges and the frame corners are pinned to points on them. A bridge thread and the frame threads go up, then the radii run from the hub to the frame, with the hub above the middle so the lower half is the bigger one. A tight mesh of irregular rings sits at the hub, an empty ring surrounds it, and the sticky spiral is laid from the outside in. Its spacing is uneven and widens toward the frame, each piece between two radii sags a little toward the hub, and the radii are tugged sideways where the spiral crosses them. In the lower sector the spiral sometimes doubles back before it carries on. Twigs, leaves and the spider hide the threads behind them, and threads cut the moon lines with a small gap so the web reads in silhouette. The stats line lists the checks: spiral pieces that do not run between two radii, hub height, anchors, pen length and build time. In Design Space: all layers to Pen, black 0.4 on the key layer, black 0.3 (or grey) on the shade layer, a coloured pen on the dew layer, Attach, Make.
