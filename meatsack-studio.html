Meatsack studio

The body as hardware: bolt on, swap out, print another. Augmentations drawn as patent sheets, a body replaced part by part, a line of clones.

Presets
The sheet

The seed picks the body's proportions, the order the augments appear in, the serial numbers and which clone is marked. The groups below each belong to one sheet; the others are ignored.

The body

Every sheet. The network grows outward from the augments and ports, so at 0 there is none and at 1 it covers the whole body. Texture goes from fine stipple, through cross-contour rings, to stitched graft seams at the top end.

Patent

Patent sheet only. With two figures the first shows the body and the second pulls the augments out along dashed lines. Every numeral on the drawing is in the parts list.

Replacement

Replacement sheet only. Parts go from the extremities inwards, hands and feet first, and the head is last.

Clones

Clone sheet only. Divergence is how far the clones have drifted: 0 identical, 0.3 posture and proportions, 0.6 each on its own augmentation path, 1 wildly diverged. In the lineage layout each generation forks and children keep what their parents grew; rows and columns set the grid, columns also cap how wide a generation may get.

Drawing

Flesh is hatched along the limb on the shadow side, light from the upper left. Replaced parts are hatched straight at 45 degrees, with seams, rivets and a joint disc.

Paper and pens

Plotter only, no cutting. Line is the 0.4 pen, shade the 0.3 pen, and the colour pen is the one chosen above. The split files carry four corner ticks so every layer lands in the same place.

computing

How it is made: each body part is a distance field (capsules, ellipses and a smooth torso outline) and its outline is traced where the field crosses zero, so neighbouring parts meet cleanly at the joints. Flesh is hatched along each limb, curving with the form, closest together on the shadow side. A replaced part keeps the same outline but gets a panel line inside it, seams with rivets, a joint disc and straight hatching, so the machine reads next to the flesh. Patent numerals are placed by a small solver that checks no two leader lines cross. The network grows outward from the augment sites as a Manhattan trace, a honeycomb or a branching vein, and the flesh hatching steps aside for it. In the clone line, each clone carries a genome of seeded mutations that build up with divergence, and the family tree lets children keep what their parents grew. Everything is drawn in the single-stroke capitals, so it all plots.
