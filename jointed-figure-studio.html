Jointed figure studio

A cut-out doll with moving joints. The pen draws each part, the blade cuts the parts out with a pin hole at every joint, and brass paper fasteners (split pins) put it together. Make a doll that poses, or a jumping jack whose arms and legs fly up when you pull the string.

Paper and pens

Split export saves one SVG per layer in job order: the pen layers, then the cut. The pen files carry four corner ticks at 1 mm and the cut file carries a trim rectangle at 1 mm, so every file lands in the same place in Design Space.

computing

How it goes together: each part is drawn as a set of rounded shapes (circles, tapered limbs, rounded boxes) merged into one outline, and every joint is a disc of card round a pin hole that sits at exactly the same point on both parts, so the parts line up when a split pin goes through both holes. Put the limbs behind the body for a jumping jack, in front for a doll. For a jumping jack, each arm and leg has a short tab past its pivot with a small string hole: tie one thread across the two arm holes and one across the two leg holes, then a pull string from the middle of both down past the feet. Pull it and the tabs go down, so the limbs go up. Hang it from the hole in the head. The pen draws first and stops a small gap short of every cut line, then the blade cuts on the same mat load; in Design Space set the pen layers to Draw, the red layer to Cut, attach everything and do not move the mat. Every cut size here is an assumption until the cut calibration sheet is read: pin hole 2.4 mm for a standard brass paper fastener, at least 3 mm of card round every hole, no part narrower than 5 mm, 3 mm between parts, ink gap 0.4 mm. The letters at the joints say which holes go together: pin A to A, B to B and so on.
