Island atlas studio

A procedural archipelago drawn as a map. Every seed is an island nobody has visited, and the same land can be drawn as a survey sheet with contours and spot heights, as an antique chart with hachures and water lines, or as a treasure map with a trail to an X. Export a millimetre SVG with up to three pens for Design Space.

Split export saves one SVG per pen (key line, shade, then colour), numbered in plotting order. Each carries the same four corner ticks so Design Space sizes them identically: place each file at the same spot on the mat, swap pens between runs, and leave the paper on the mat.

computing

The land is a height grid of seeded noise with domain warping, shaped by a falloff for one island, several, an atoll, a volcano or a delta. Rivers start at high points and follow steepest descent over a pit-filled copy of the grid, so each one ends at the sea or in a lake, and they are carved into the ground lightly afterwards. Where a river carries a large share of the catchment it is drawn as two bank lines. The survey style draws contours, with every fifth on the key pen, plus spot heights in a small single-stroke digit font and dashed depth contours in the sea. The antique style draws hachures that follow the downhill direction, hatched mountain glyphs, water lines that grow further apart offshore and stop where another island's lines meet them, and a portolan net of rhumb lines that stops at every coast. The treasure style adds a least-cost trail to an X. Contours, water lines and hachures are thinned so no two lines lie nearer than the hatch pitch. The scale bar assumes 1 mm on paper is 0.625 km. In Design Space: send the layers to Pen, black 0.4 for the key line, black 0.3 for the shade, and a colour pen for water or rhumb lines, Attach, Make.
