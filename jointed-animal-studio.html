Jointed animal studio

Cut-out animals with moving legs. The pen draws a rabbit, horse, dog, cat, elephant or pig in side view, the blade cuts it into a body, a head that nods, a tail that wags and four legs, and brass paper fasteners pin it together. Give it spots, stripes or patches: a striped horse is a zebra, a spotty dog a dalmatian.

Paper and pens

Split export saves one SVG per layer in job order: the pen layers, then the cut. The pen files carry four corner ticks at 1 mm and the cut file carries a trim rectangle at 1 mm, so every file lands in the same place in Design Space.

computing

How it goes together: every part is a few rounded shapes merged into one outline, and every joint is a disc of card round a pin hole at the same point on both parts. The two front legs share one split pin at the shoulder and the two back legs share one at the hip: far leg at the back, then the body, then the near leg in front, so each of those pins holds three layers of card. The head pins at the neck and the tail at the rump, both behind the body. With knees on, each leg has a second pin at the knee. Letters say which holes go together: A shoulders, B hips, C and D front knees, E and F back knees, N the neck, T the tail. The pen draws first and stops a small gap short of every cut line, then the blade cuts on the same mat load. Every cut size is an assumption until the cut calibration sheet is read: pin hole 2.4 mm for a brass paper fastener, at least 3 mm of card round every hole, no part narrower than 5 mm, 3 mm between parts, ink gap 0.4 mm. Markings are seeded, so the same seed gives the same spots.
