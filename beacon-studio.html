Beacon studio

Two ways to shout into the dark. A message to the stars: an Arecibo-style bitmap whose width and height are both prime, sent from a dish as rings that carry its bits, with a Dyson swarm far off that may open a window and answer. Or a LORAN chart: radio beacons on a generated coast, their time differences drawn as lanes of hyperbolas, a ship fixing itself where two lanes cross. Day 22 of Inktober, "beacon".

computing

How it is made. The message is built section by section on a grid whose width and height are both prime, so whoever receives 1,679 bits (or however many) can only lay them out one way: in 1974 Arecibo sent 23 x 73. It counts 1 to 10 in binary, gives the atomic numbers of hydrogen, carbon, nitrogen, oxygen and phosphorus, a helix, a figure with its height and a population beside it, the sun and planets with home raised a row, and the dish itself; the shapes here are generated from the seed, not copied. The rings are arcs round the dish's feed, and in "carries the bits" each ring is the message itself, read row by row as long and short dashes. The swarm is collectors on tilted rings round a star; when it answers, the collectors facing us part into a round window and its reply comes back as rings of its own. The LORAN lanes are exact: every point on one line has the same difference in distance to the master and to one secondary, which is a hyperbola with the two stations as its foci, and on the baseline between them the lanes sit half a step apart. Two lanes crossing give a fix. With the waves showing, the rings of two stations cross on exactly those lines.
