# Hooray cards

**Version:** 5 · 2026-10-13

Inktober day 15, prompt "hooray". One studio, `hooray-card-studio.html`. Round 4 starts again on two ideas: the word reads through a change of pattern, and the offcuts are the confetti. Round 5 drops the words on the back, cuts more confetti there instead, draws on every piece, adds more ink, and puts the message on its own card.

The ink stays 0.65 mm inside the cut.

The back panel gets four diagonal slits at its corners; tuck the card's corners in from inside. With the card off, an inside page is glued in as before. The preview has a new view, the inside, showing the card in its slits.

## The idea

- **Two patterns.** The front is covered in one pen pattern. HOORAY is cut through it, and the coloured paper glued behind carries a second pattern. The word shows as a change of texture as well as of colour.
- **Layers.** With three layers, the front's letters are cut a step bigger than the middle layer's. Each letter gets a rim in the middle layer's pattern, and the back pattern shows inside it. Foam tape between the layers gives real depth.
- **The offcuts are the confetti.** Small confetti shapes are cut from the front around the word. Every piece the blade lifts out (the letters and the confetti) goes into a printed pocket glued inside the card. The pocket's small print gives the real count: "20 pieces of your card".

## Patterns

None, diagonal hatching, pinstripes, crosshatch, graph paper, waves, zigzag, polka dots, sunburst rays, rings, confetti and sparkles. Each layer has its own pattern, spacing, pen (black 0.4 or grey 0.3) and preview paper. Rays and rings centre on the word. The sunburst's rays start staggered, so they are never closer than 0.8 mm and never pile into a blob.

## Pieces and sheets

- **The front:** the folded card, with the pattern, the windows, the score line, and TO and FROM on the back.
- **The layers behind:** the middle and back layers, the same size as the front panel.
- **Inside page and pocket:** the message, with corner marks for the pocket. The pocket is a net with side flaps and a top flap; the label panel stays put, so its words read the right way up.
- **The envelope:** its flaps shrink to fit the 12 x 12 mat where they must, with a note if they get too small to work.

## How it goes together

1. Plot black, then grey, then score, then cut (split export, in job order, with matching corner ticks). Each layer goes on its own paper.
2. Glue the back layer behind the front panel. With three layers, the middle goes first, on foam tape if you want depth.
3. Tip every offcut into the pocket, fold it on its score lines, and glue it inside on the corner marks.

## Gates (every build)

- Front and middle windows: at least 4 mm inside the edge and at least 2 mm apart.
- Confetti at least 4 mm across; a shape that would come out smaller is scaled up to that.
- Pen ink at least 0.4 mm from every cut on its own layer, checked on every point.
- Pen length under 150 m and the SVG under 3 MB.
- Pieces at least 3 mm apart and inside the margin, and the trim frame matching the corner ticks.

MEASURED: all six presets pass on all four sheets. A fuzz of 800 random settings fails 1, a 0.8 mm pattern over a whole layer that makes a 4.3 MB file, which the size gate rightly flags.

## Fitting the word

A line's width is linear in the cap height, so the studio solves for the biggest cap that fits instead of iterating. It tries one, two and three lines. With three layers, if a long word would leave letters under 9 mm, it eases the step down to 1.2 mm and says so. A counter too small to keep (a heavy G nearly closing up) is cut away with its letter. If a bridge would leave two pieces of one letter closer than 2 mm, that letter loses its bridges.
